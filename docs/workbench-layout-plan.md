## Zone Organization

Your workbench should have 4 distinct zones:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                           WALL / PEGBOARD                                  │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                            │
│   ZONE 1         ZONE 2           ZONE 3          ZONE 4                  │
│   SOLDERING      ASSEMBLY         TESTING         STORAGE                 │
│   (18" wide)     (24" wide)       (18" wide)      (12" wide)              │
│                                                                            │
│   - Iron         - Breadboards    - Multimeter    - Parts drawers         │
│   - Solder       - Active project - Power supply  - ESP32 boards          │
│   - Flux         - Components     - Logic analyzer- Sensors               │
│   - Stand        - Wire/tools     - USB meter     - Modules               │
│   - Magnifier    - Helping hands  - Cables        - Tools overflow        │
│                                                                            │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Zone 1: Soldering (Left 18")
**Purpose:** All hot-work activities
**Key items:**
- Soldering iron in stand
- Solder wire, flux, tip cleaner
- Heat-resistant silicone mat
- Fume extractor (desktop unit)
- Magnifying lamp (can swing over other zones)

**Why left side:** Right-handed people naturally reach right for components, left for hot iron (keeps hot tool away from active work)
**If left-handed:** Mirror this layout (soldering on right)

### Zone 2: Assembly (Center-left 24")
**Purpose:** Active project work and breadboarding
**Key items:**
- Current project(s) on breadboards
- Helping hands / PCB holder
- Component bins (active project parts)
- Wire spools and jumper wires
- Hand tools (cutters, strippers, pliers)
- ESD mat covers this zone primarily

**Why center:** Most time spent here, needs most space

### Zone 3: Testing (Center-right 18")
**Purpose:** Powering, measuring, debugging
**Key items:**
- Bench power supply
- Multimeter with probes
- Logic analyzer
- USB power meter
- Cables (USB, power, test leads)
- USB hub for programming ESP32 boards

**Why right side:** Separation from hot soldering work, close to assembly zone

### Zone 4: Storage (Right 12")
**Purpose:** Quick-access component storage
**Key items:**
- Small parts organizer (20-40 drawer unit)
- Bin of ESP32 boards
- Frequently-used sensors/modules
- Tool overflow (screwdrivers, tweezers)
- Anti-static bags

**Why far right:** Out of the way but accessible, frees up center for work

## Lighting Setup

### Requirements for Electronics Work

**Target illumination:** 500-1000 lux (50-100 foot-candles)
**Color temperature:** 5000K-6500K (daylight white)
**CRI (Color Rendering Index):** 80+ (for seeing color-coded wires/components)

### Multi-Layer Lighting Approach

#### Layer 1: Overhead Task Lighting (Primary)
**Purpose:** General workspace illumination

**Option A: LED Shop Light** ($30-50)
- **Type:** 4' linkable LED shop light, 4000 lumens
- **Mounting:** Hang from ceiling directly above workbench, 4-5 feet above desk surface
- **Where:** Home Depot, Canadian Tire, Amazon.ca
- **Models:** Hyperikon, Lithonia, Barrina

**Option B: LED Strip Kit** ($25-40)
- **Type:** High-CRI LED strip, 12V, 5000K, 600 LEDs/meter
- **Mounting:** Aluminum channel mounted to bottom of shelf above desk
- **Where:** Amazon.ca
- **Pros:** Sleek, no shadows, adjustable positioning

#### Layer 2: Magnifying Lamp (Task Lighting)
**Purpose:** Close-up soldering and inspection work

**Recommended:** Desktop clamp magnifying lamp with LED ring
- **Magnification:** 5x to 10x (adjustable or interchangeable lens)
- **Arm:** Articulating arm, 24-30" reach
- **Clamp:** Mounts to left front edge of desk
- **Where:** Amazon.ca, DigiKey.ca
- **Price:** $40-80
- **Models:** OttLite, Brightech, Luxo

**Usage:**
- Position over Zone 1 (soldering) for close work
- Swing over Zone 2 (assembly) for breadboard work
- Fold away when not needed

#### Layer 3: Desk Lamp (Supplemental)
**Purpose:** Fill-in lighting, adjustable beam

**Type:** LED desk lamp, adjustable arm, 800+ lumens
- **Mounting:** Clamp or weighted base on right rear of desk
- **Where:** Ikea, Amazon.ca, Canadian Tire
- **Price:** $25-50
- **Bonus features:** Dimmable, USB charging port

## Ventilation Plan

### Why Ventilation Matters
- Solder flux fumes are respiratory irritants
- Prolonged exposure can cause sensitization
- Beginner = longer soldering sessions (more fumes)

### Three-Tier Ventilation Strategy

#### Tier 1: Passive Ventilation (Free)
**Setup:**
- Position workbench near window
- Open window during soldering sessions
- Use small desk fan to blow fumes toward window

**Effectiveness:** 40-60% fume reduction (acceptable for occasional use)

#### Tier 2: Desktop Fume Extractor ($40-60) **RECOMMENDED**
**Setup:**
- Position 6-12" behind soldering iron
- Angled to pull fumes away from breathing zone
- Replaceable activated carbon filter

**Recommended Units:**
- Hakko FA-400 (~$200, professional)
- Generic desktop fume extractor (~$50, Amazon.ca)

**DIY Option ($15-25):**
- 120mm PC fan (high CFM)
- Activated carbon filter (cut to size)
- 3D printed or cardboard housing
- 12V power supply
- **Guide:** Plenty of YouTube tutorials for "DIY solder fume extractor"

**Effectiveness:** 80-90% fume reduction (excellent for home use)

#### Tier 3: Professional Extraction ($100+)
**Setup:**
- Fume extraction arm with high-CFM fan
- HEPA + carbon filtration
- Ducted to outside window

**Only needed if:**
- Soldering 4+ hours per day
- Production work (building many boards)
- Shared workspace (family members, pets nearby)

### Ventilation Layout

```
                    WINDOW (open during soldering)
                        │
                        ↑ Airflow direction
                        │
    ┌───────────────────┼────────────────────────┐
    │ PEGBOARD          │                        │
    ├───────────────────┼────────────────────────┤
    │                   │                        │
    │  [Soldering]  [Fume                        │
    │    Iron  →→→  Extractor]                   │
    │               │   ↑                         │
    │               │   │ Pulls fumes away       │
    │          Heat mat │ from face              │
    │                   │                         │
    │                                             │
    │                YOU (sitting here)           │
    │                   ↓ Breathe clean air       │
    └─────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Position: Fume extractor 8-12" behind and 4-6" above soldering work
Airflow: Pulls fumes backward, away from your face, toward window/filter
```

**Setup tips:**
- Test with flux smoke before starting (verify airflow direction)
- Adjust height/distance based on room airflow
- Change carbon filters every 6-12 months (more often with heavy use)

## Ergonomics

### Desk Height
**Standard sitting:** 28-30" (most desks)
**Standing:** 36-42" (adjustable with stool)

**Test:** When seated, elbows should be at 90° angle when hands on desk

### Chair
**Requirements:**
- Adjustable height
- Back support (lumbar)
- Armrests (optional but helpful for long sessions)
- Swivel base (reach all zones without twisting)

**Recommended:** Office chair (not kitchen chair) - check Ikea, Staples, used office furniture stores

### Seating Position
```
        HEAD: Upright, not hunched
         │
    SHOULDERS: Relaxed, not raised
         │
    ── ELBOWS: 90° angle ──
         │
      WRISTS: Neutral (not bent)
         │
      HANDS: Comfortable on desk
         │
      BACK: Supported by chair
         │
      HIPS: 90-100° angle
         │
      FEET: Flat on floor or footrest
```

### Preventing Repetitive Strain

**Every 45-60 minutes:**
- Stand up, walk around (2-3 minutes)
- Stretch wrists and fingers
- Look at distant object (rest eyes from close work)
- Roll shoulders back

**Wrist stretches:**
1. Extend arm, pull fingers back (hold 10 seconds)
2. Make fist, rotate wrist clockwise/counter-clockwise (10 rotations each)
3. Prayer stretch (palms together, lower hands while keeping palms touching)

### Magnification vs. Posture
**Problem:** Hunching over to see small components
**Solution:** Use magnifying lamp (5x-10x) instead of leaning in
**Benefit:** Better posture, less neck/back strain, better solder joints

- Wall-mounted fold-down table (IKEA Norbo or DIY)
- Fold up when not in use (saves floor space)
- Pegboard permanently mounted above (always accessible)
- Rolling cart for storage (wheels under, pull out when working)

**Pros:** Saves space in small apartments/rooms
**Cons:** Setup/teardown each session (5-10 minutes)

### Corner L-Shaped Desk
**Setup:**
- Use corner of room (maximizes space efficiency)
- L-shape: 4' on each wall = 8' total working length
- One side for soldering/assembly, other side for testing/storage

**Pros:** Large work area in small footprint
**Cons:** Harder to organize into linear zones

## Troubleshooting Common Setup Issues

### "I don't have a wall near my desk for pegboard"
**Solution:** Free-standing pegboard on desk (buy pegboard with legs/stand, or build simple frame)

### "My desk is too small for 4 zones"
**Solution:** Combine Zones 1-2 (soldering/assembly share space), put testing elsewhere (rolling cart)

### "I can't mount overhead lighting"
**Solution:** Use 2 desk lamps instead (one each side), or LED strip on adjustable arm

### "My room has no window for ventilation"
**Solution:**
- Buy fume extractor with carbon filter (doesn't need ducting)
- Use door + fan to create airflow to hallway
- Consider working in different room (kitchen table with portable setup)

### "I'm renting and can't drill holes for pegboard"
**Solution:**
- Command strips (heavy-duty) for lightweight pegboard
- Free-standing pegboard on desk
- Magnetic tool board (if metal wall/surface available)
- Rolling tool cart instead
